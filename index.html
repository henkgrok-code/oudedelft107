A bit of history

You are about to live on one of the oldest canals in the Netherlands. A few things to look out for on your walk home:

  • 1100

    The oldest canal in Delft. The Oude Delft was probably dug around the year 1100, before the town of Delft even existed, to drain the surrounding land.

  • Jan

    The leaning tower. The Oude Kerk, founded in 1246, has a 75-metre brick tower that leans about two metres. Locals call it “Scheve Jan”, Skewed John.

  • 1584

    The Prinsenhof. Just down the canal, at Oude Delft 175, William of Orange lived in a former convent. He was shot there in 1584, and you can still see the bullet holes in the wall.

  • Gold

    Merchants’ houses. In the Golden Age wealthy merchants settled on this higher ground, and many of their canal houses along the Oude Delft are now national monuments (rijksmonumenten).

  • Art

    Vermeer’s Delft. The painter Johannes Vermeer was buried in the Oude Kerk in 1675, and so was the microscope pioneer Antonie van Leeuwenhoek in 1723.

  • 1842

    A student street. Delft’s university began in 1842 as a Royal Academy, and the former main building of TU Delft stands at Oude Delft 95. Several student societies still have their clubhouses on this canal, so you are in good company.

Sources: Wikipedia (Oude Delft; Sint-Agathaklooster/Prinsenhof; Oude Kerk; TU Delft).
